# K-Detect Model Card

**Project:** K-Detect

**Version:** 0.2.0 (Research Evaluation)

**Status:** Experimental research project

**Last updated:** November 2026

## 1. Overview

K-Detect is an independent research project investigating how machine-learning methods distinguish human-written, AI-revised, and AI-generated scientific writing.

The project evaluates existing text-classification approaches, studies their limitations, and examines how detection performance changes across datasets and text-generation systems.

K-Detect is a research and evaluation framework, not a validated production-grade authorship detector.

## 2. Intended Use

The project is intended for:

- Academic research into AI-generated scientific writing.

- Educational exploration of linguistic and textual patterns.

- Comparative evaluation of traditional machine-learning classifiers.

- Investigation of cross-dataset generalization.

- Analysis of false positives, false negatives, and classification uncertainty.

- Research into the limitations and potential biases of automated AI-text detection.

It is not intended to determine authorship conclusively or make academic-integrity decisions.

## 3. Application Capabilities

The Streamlit application provides descriptive academic writing analysis, including:

- Word and sentence counts.

- Vocabulary diversity estimates.

- Average sentence length.

- Sentence-length variation.

- Repeated-word patterns.

- Selected formulaic expressions and transitions.

- TXT and DOCX document input.

- Downloadable analysis reports.

These linguistic measurements describe observable text characteristics. They do not independently establish whether a document was written by a human or generated by AI.

The research classifiers described below are maintained as separate experimental components.

## 4. Experimental Classification Models

The research pipeline evaluates three-class classification:

| Label | Class | Description |

|---|---|---|

| 0 | Human-generated (HGT) | Human-written scientific abstracts |

| 1 | AI-revised (ART) | Human-written abstracts revised using AI |

| 2 | AI-generated (AGT) | AI-generated scientific abstracts |

The evaluated approaches include:

### 4.1 TF-IDF Logistic Regression

- Word-level TF-IDF representation.

- Unigrams and bigrams.

- Logistic Regression classifier.

- Three-class probability outputs.

### 4.2 Character-level SVM

- Character-level TF-IDF representation.

- Character n-grams.

- Linear Support Vector Classification.

- Probability calibration for experimental probability-based comparisons.

### 4.3 Equal-weight Ensemble

- Combines the word-level and character-level model probabilities.

- Uses equal weighting.

- Evaluated as an experimental alternative, not an established improvement over individual models.

## 5. Evaluation Datasets

### 5.1 SciHRA-Detect

The primary scientific-writing dataset used in the classification experiments.

It contains human-generated, AI-revised, and AI-generated scientific abstracts across multiple research domains.

The processed dataset was divided using article-level grouping:

- Training: 7,200 abstracts from 2,400 articles.

- Validation: 900 abstracts from 300 articles.

- Test: 900 abstracts from 300 articles.

Article IDs were kept separate across the three splits.

The evaluation does not establish generalization to entirely unseen source collections, authors, or domains.

### 5.2 RAID External Evaluation

A non-adversarial subset of the RAID benchmark was used for external evaluation.

The subset contains human scientific abstracts and AI-generated outputs from multiple generator families and decoding conditions.

The RAID evaluation is an external benchmark-subset experiment, not an evaluation on the official hidden RAID test set.

The RAID experiment examines whether classifiers trained on SciHRA retain their performance across different text-generation systems.

## 6. Reported Experimental Results

### 6.1 SciHRA Held-out Test Results

| Model | Accuracy | Macro F1 |

|---|---:|---:|

| TF-IDF Logistic Regression | 93.67% | 93.63% |

| Character-level SVM | 95.78% | 95.78% |

| Equal-weight ensemble | 96.00% | 96.00% |

The ensemble showed a small numerical improvement over the character-level SVM.

However, paired article-level bootstrap confidence intervals included zero for the observed improvement. Therefore, the experiment did not establish a conclusive advantage for the ensemble.

These results apply only to the specified SciHRA dataset and experimental split.

### 6.2 RAID External Evaluation

External evaluation revealed substantial generator-specific variation.

The word-level Logistic Regression model achieved stronger average performance than the character-level model and equal-weight ensemble under the evaluated conditions.

Performance varied considerably across generator families. Some generators were detected with relatively high F1 scores, while others were frequently misclassified.

These findings indicate that strong performance on one scientific-writing dataset does not guarantee reliable detection across different generation systems.

The results should not be interpreted as universal real-world detection accuracy.

## 7. Evaluation Methods

The project includes:

- Article-level grouped data splitting.

- Accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score.

- Balanced accuracy and ROC-AUC.

- False positive and false negative rates.

- Confusion-matrix analysis.

- Bootstrap confidence intervals.

- Paired article-level model comparisons.

- Feature ablation.

- Feature-frequency and importance analysis.

- Scientific-domain comparisons.

- Generator-specific external evaluation.

- Greedy and sampling decoding-condition comparisons.

The RAID evaluation reuses human source documents across generation conditions. Statistical comparisons therefore account for source-level grouping where applicable.

## 8. Bias and Fairness Considerations

AI-text detection may behave differently across:

- Native and non-native English writing.

- Different English proficiency levels.

- Academic disciplines.

- Writing styles and levels of editing.

- AI-generation systems and decoding conditions.

- Human-written and AI-revised scientific text.

The current experiments do not establish fairness across all language backgrounds, disciplines, or populations.

Dataset-specific linguistic patterns may influence classifier predictions. Feature importance or feature ablation results should not be interpreted as universal AI-writing fingerprints.

## 9. Limitations

K-Detect has important limitations:

- The classifiers are experimental and are not validated for high-stakes use.

- Performance may change substantially across datasets and generators.

- AI-generated text may be misclassified as human-written.

- Human-written text may be incorrectly flagged as AI-generated.

- Editing, paraphrasing, translation, and changes in writing style may affect predictions.

- Short documents may provide insufficient evidence for reliable classification.

- Probability estimates calibrated on one dataset may not remain calibrated on another.

- The current experiments do not establish broad generalization to all academic disciplines or writing populations.

- Third-party datasets may have provenance, licensing, and redistribution restrictions.

No classifier score should be treated as proof of authorship.
